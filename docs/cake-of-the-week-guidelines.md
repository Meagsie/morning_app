# Cake of the Week — Kitchen Guidelines

_Owner: Megan · Last updated: 16 August 2026_

## The one rule that matters right now

**Until we are consistently selling more than one cake a week, the Cake of the
Week must be able to live on the countertop for the full week — or at an
absolute minimum, four to five days — without needing refrigeration.**

If a cake has to be kept in the fridge, it is not a candidate for Cake of the
Week yet. It can absolutely come back onto the list once our volume grows (see
"When this changes" below).

## Why — this isn't a preference, it's seven years of counter data

I've stood at the front counter, beside the cakes, for seven years. Here is what
I've watched happen, over and over:

- **Cake is an emotional, visual purchase.** People very rarely walk in
  intending to buy a cake. They see it, and *then* they want it. If it isn't out
  where they can see it, that sale never starts.
- **A whole cake sells. A lonely slice does not.** A full, generous cake on the
  stand reads as luxurious, decadent, and abundant. That's what makes someone
  reach for it.
- **The "last piece" problem.** When there's a single slice on the plate because
  the rest is in the fridge, customers assume it's the leftover, the old one,
  the one nobody wanted. They pass on it — even when it's perfectly fresh.
- **People eat with their eyes first.** Refrigeration takes the cake out of
  sight, and out of sight means out of the sale.

A cake that has to be refrigerated spends its life hidden, and comes out one sad
slice at a time. That is the fastest way to guarantee we keep selling one cake a
week instead of growing.

## What this means in practice

**Good candidates** — cakes that hold their look and quality on the counter for
4–5+ days:

- Buttercream, ganache, and glaze finishes
- Oil-based and syrup-soaked cakes (carrot, banana, orange, coffee, etc.)
- Loaf and bundt styles that stay moist under a dome
- Anything we've historically been able to leave out and keep beautiful

**Not right now** — beautiful, but fridge-dependent:

- Fresh cream / whipped cream fillings and toppings
- Mascarpone and cream-cheese icings that must be kept cold
- Mousse, custard, or fresh-fruit-topped cakes that won't hold at room temp

> Rob's banana cake with mascarpone icing is a genuinely lovely bake — this
> isn't about the skill. It's that the mascarpone forces it into the fridge, and
> the fridge is where cake sales go to die at our current volume. If there's a
> countertop-stable icing that gets us close to that flavour, that's the version
> I want on the stand.

## When this changes

This is a volume rule, not a forever rule. Once we're reliably moving cake fast
enough that a whole one is gone well before it could spoil on the counter, the
"must survive the week out" constraint relaxes — and fridge-kept, cream-based
showstoppers come back onto the menu. **The goal is to earn our way to that
point, and full, visible, countertop cakes are how we get there.**

## The short version for the pass

- Whole cake, out on the stand, looking generous — always.
- Countertop-stable for 4–5 days minimum until sales grow.
- No fridge-only cakes as Cake of the Week for now.
- Gorgeous but fridge-dependent bakes: park them, don't bin the idea.
