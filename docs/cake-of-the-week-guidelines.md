# Cake of the Week — Kitchen Guidelines

_Owner: Megan · Last updated: 26 August 2026_

## The one rule that matters right now

**Until we are consistently selling more than one cake a week, the Cake of the
Week must be able to live on the countertop for the full week — or at an
absolute minimum, four to five days — without needing refrigeration.**

If a cake has to be kept in the fridge, it is not a candidate for Cake of the
Week yet. It can come back once our volume grows (see "When this changes").

## What the last two weeks told us

- **Simple outsold fancy.** The plainer cake sold better than the iced cake the
  following week — even with the iced one out on the counter. Fussy isn't what
  moves cake here; a beautiful, simple, whole cake is.
- **Scones are done.** They don't sell, they have no shelf life, and many of our
  women can bake something like that at home themselves. That makes them a waste
  of labour and ingredients. **Do not put scones out again.**

## Why the countertop rule holds — seven years of counter data

I've stood at the front counter, beside the cakes, for seven years:

- **Cake is an emotional, visual purchase.** People rarely walk in intending to
  buy cake. They see it, and *then* they want it. Not on display = sale never
  starts.
- **A whole cake sells; a lonely slice does not.** A full cake reads as
  luxurious, decadent, and generous. A single slice reads as the old one, the
  leftover, the one nobody wanted.
- **Fridge = hidden = out of sight = out of sale.** People eat with their eyes
  first. Refrigeration takes the cake off the stand, and that's where the sale
  dies at our current volume.

## Two things that carry equal weight: consistency and quality

**Consistency.** Sales won't jump overnight — this takes time and a *regular,
reliable* offering. The women need to know that any day they bring guests in,
there will be a delicious cake waiting. That reliability is what grows the
habit, and the habit is what grows sales. A cake that's there some weeks and not
others never builds that trust.

**Quality.** Simple does not mean plain or lesser. Simple means beautifully made.
There is no point putting out a cake that isn't gorgeous — a great countertop
cake still has to look and taste exceptional. Simple + beautiful is the target,
every single time.

## What this means in practice

**Good candidates** — simple, beautiful, and hold their quality on the counter
for 4–5+ days:

- Buttercream, ganache, and glaze finishes
- Oil-based and syrup-soaked cakes (carrot, banana, orange, coffee, etc.)
- Loaf and bundt styles that stay moist under a dome
- Anything we can leave out and keep looking beautiful all week

**Not right now** — fridge-dependent, and cut items:

- Fresh / whipped cream, mascarpone and cream-cheese icings (fridge-bound)
- Mousse, custard, or fresh-fruit-topped cakes that won't hold at room temp
- **Scones — discontinued**

## When this changes

This is a volume rule, not a forever rule. Once we're reliably moving cake fast
enough that a whole one is gone before it could spoil on the counter, the
fridge-kept, cream-based showstoppers come back onto the menu. **A consistent,
beautiful, countertop cake — week after week — is how we earn our way there.**

## The short version for the pass

- Whole cake, out on the stand, looking generous — always.
- Simple, but beautifully made. Never put out anything less than gorgeous.
- Countertop-stable for 4–5 days minimum until sales grow.
- Same reliable offering every week so guests can count on it.
- No fridge-only cakes as Cake of the Week for now. No scones.
